\begin{abstract}
Acoustic gunshot detection on low-cost edge platforms introduces significant physical challenges, including transducer domain shift, diaphragm saturation, and false-positive triggers from percussive environmental noises. Most existing solutions rely on continuous streaming of raw audio to centralized servers, which compromises civilian privacy and incurs high bandwidth costs. This paper presents a robust, decentralized detection architecture engineered specifically for localized edge deployment on a Raspberry Pi. By processing acoustic data entirely on the sensor node, our system guarantees privacy by transmitting only streamlined inference telemetry to the central gateway. To combat hardware-induced signal corruption, we integrate rigorous signal conditioning protocols directly into the embedded pipeline, alongside Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) to stabilize dynamic microphone gain fluctuations. We empirically evaluate multiple neural architectures, transitioning from traditional 1D classifiers to optimized 2D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs). Our findings demonstrate that while classical machine learning classifiers fail catastrophically during live streaming, our enhanced deep learning ensemble successfully isolates the microsecond shockwave signatures of firearms from complex background noise. The proposed architecture maintains strict real-time inference latency and exceptional resilience against transducer variability, establishing a highly secure, autonomous benchmark for IoT-based public safety networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The integration of the Internet of Things (IoT) into public safety infrastructure introduces critical preventative measures and rapid threat detection capabilities essential for modern security architectures. Automated acoustic gunshot detection serves as a foundational layer of defense, capable of autonomously identifying and locating firearm discharges in real-time. By continuously analyzing environmental audio at the edge, these decentralized sensor networks generate immediate telemetry for emergency responders, thereby drastically reducing response latency compared to traditional dispatch channels. Operating strictly within the acoustic domain, such systems intrinsically preserve civilian privacy by circumventing the necessity for pervasive video surveillance. The strategic deployment of autonomous acoustic sensors across urban grids, educational campuses, and wildlife sanctuaries establishes an active monitoring topology that is highly effective for immediate threat containment and subsequent forensic analysis.

Existing methodologies for acoustic gunshot detection have historically relied upon classical digital signal processing and heuristic energy thresholding, techniques that are notoriously susceptible to environmental noise and hardware variability. Foundational studies characterized the acoustic signature of a gunshot as the convolution of a supersonic projectile shockwave and a prolonged muzzle blast decay \cite{maher2007}. Subsequent systems introduced conventional machine learning classifiers operating on standard audio features, such as Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs). While city-scale proprietary systems transmit raw audio to centralized cloud servers for computationally intensive processing, embedded baselines have demonstrated the feasibility of localized inference on resource-constrained platforms, such as the Raspberry Pi \cite{valero2020}. The advent of specialized acoustic datasets \cite{dib2023, salamon2014} has catalyzed the training of deep learning architectures, such as spectrogram-based Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which have proven effective in constrained environments like forested terrains \cite{saha2025}. Compared to these existing implementations, the architecture proposed herein fundamentally elevates real-world reliability by directly addressing hardware-induced signal corruption at the edge, exploiting advanced bioacoustic normalization techniques.

Despite the proliferation of machine learning in audio classification, significant research gaps persist regarding the deployment of robust acoustic anomaly detection on highly constrained edge devices. The majority of contemporary models are trained on pristine, studio-quality datasets, resulting in catastrophic failure when exposed to the stochastic nature of live microphone streams, which are afflicted by unpredictable transducer domain shifts, DC voltage rail drift, and thermal ADC hiss. Furthermore, traditional systems frequently fail to differentiate true firearm discharges from percussive imposters---such as fireworks, door slams, and thunder---due to inadequate temporal resolution and rudimentary feature extraction methodologies. The failure to systematically account for microphone saturation and acoustic clipping severely restricts the generalizability of current edge-deployed detectors, emphasizing the critical necessity for a system engineered explicitly for heterogeneous, low-fidelity sensor nodes.

The methodology proposed in this work resolves these critical vulnerabilities by introducing a multi-paradigm neural architecture specifically optimized for localized edge inference. By transitioning from standard log-mel spectrograms to Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) coupled with a Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN), the system achieves unprecedented stability against transient background noise and dynamic microphone gain fluctuations. The operational inference window is strategically expanded to 750\,ms to comprehensively capture the acoustic reverberation decay, a crucial feature for distinguishing true gunshots from transient environmental knocks. Furthermore, the system integrates rigorous signal conditioning protocols---including sample mean DC removal and energy-gated peak scaling---directly into the embedded inference pipeline. This paradigm ensures that the underlying CNN and CRNN models operate exclusively on normalized, scale-invariant features, effectively immunizing the detector against the hardware-level anomalies that typically derail traditional embedded systems.

This research surpasses contemporary edge-based detection frameworks by demonstrating superior ambient specificity and imposter rejection without compromising critical inference speed. The primary contributions of this paper include the empirical evaluation of both 1D and 2D neural architectures under strict TinyML hardware constraints \cite{banbury2021}, culminating in an Enhanced 2D CNN that executes in 0.09\,ms with a minimal 124\,KB INT8 footprint on a Raspberry Pi platform. Additionally, the integration of a multi-model ensemble raises the unseen gunshot recall to 77\% while executing well within stringent real-time hop boundaries. The proposed architecture fundamentally eliminates reliance on cloud-based audio streaming, instead transmitting strictly streamlined, highly compressed inference telemetry to the network gateway. This decentralized edge-processing approach ensures maximum privacy preservation and operational resilience, establishing a new benchmark for autonomous, device-level acoustic threat detection.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The open-source firearm recordings utilized in this research incorporate the multi-caliber, multi-orientation dataset curated in \emph{Data in Brief}~\cite{dib2023}. Ambient and imposter audio samples were drawn from comprehensive urban environmental recordings~\cite{salamon2014, piczak2015, fonseca2021} and large-scale audio event ontologies~\cite{gemmeke2017}. Acoustic gunshot detection systems, such as those detailed by Valero-G{\'o}mez et al.~\cite{valero2020}, have demonstrated that localized detection on hardware akin to the Raspberry Pi is operationally viable. However, the majority of large-scale commercial systems persistently stream raw audio to centralized servers. A secure, private campus node cannot adopt this paradigm: both bandwidth limitations and strict privacy constraints dictate that classification decisions must be executed exclusively on the edge device within the sound wave domain. Spectrogram-based CNNs have emerged as a standard methodology for audio classification~\cite{hershey2017} and have been successfully applied to automated gunshot detection in forest environments~\cite{saha2025}. On constrained microcontrollers, the primary architectural limits encompass flash memory, RAM, and inference latency under integer arithmetic~\cite{banbury2021, david2021}.

To effectively manage continuous environmental noise, Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) divides each frequency band by a temporally smoothed estimate of its localized background~\cite{lostanlen2019}. Under this transformation, stationary noises such as steady rain, wind, and ambient hum collapse toward a constant value, whereas short impulsive transients remain salient. Lostanlen et al.\ originally adapted the PCEN formulation for bioacoustic monitoring, specifically for detecting chainsaws in highly adverse outdoor rainforest conditions~\cite{lostanlen2019taslp}. The CRNN architecture presented in this paper leverages this exact bioacoustic front-end to isolate gunshot shockwaves. Concurrently, Dai et al.\ demonstrated that wide 1D convolutional kernels operating on raw waveforms can accurately capture short impulsive transients~\cite{dai2017}. The gated recurrent unit (GRU) employed in our CRNN adheres to the standard formulation~\cite{cho2014}. The enhanced models investigated in this study utilize MixUp~\cite{zhang2018}, SpecAugment~\cite{park2019}, and speed perturbation~\cite{ko2015} exclusively as regularization strategies applied directly to the acoustic signals.

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system_model}

The end-to-end system architecture operates entirely at the edge, converting raw acoustic waveforms into streamlined, actionable threat alerts. The block diagram of the proposed system model and edge processing pipeline is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig1_edge_pipeline.pdf}
\caption{End-to-end edge processing pipeline. The 750\,ms acoustic frame is conditioned and transformed into time-frequency representations directly on the embedded node. Quantized INT8 inference is executed locally, and only a streamlined detection telemetry packet is transmitted via the radio interface.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

The node's microphone continuously buffers ambient audio. When an impulsive transient exceeds the hardware energy threshold, a 750\,ms operational window is isolated. The raw waveform undergoes immediate signal conditioning to strip DC offsets and apply energy-gated peak scaling, intrinsically correcting for inexpensive hardware variances. The normalized signal is transformed into the time-frequency domain utilizing either log-mel spectrograms or PCEN. These extracted features are subsequently passed into the quantized INT8 neural network (e.g., Enhanced 2D CNN or CRNN-PCEN) executing on the Raspberry Pi's processor. Upon a positive detection, the system aggregates the confidence score and localized timestamp, transmitting only this highly streamlined telemetry to the central gateway, entirely bypassing the requirement to transmit raw audio payloads.

\section{Data Pipeline and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

The initial dataset corpus comprised approximately 80,000 audio samples, integrating the calibrated firearm set~\cite{dib2023}, 4,138 supplementary firearm recordings from diverse open-ground and indoor environments, and 6,230 extended ambient recordings (encompassing traffic, rain, construction, and speech)~\cite{salamon2014, piczak2015, fonseca2021}, ranging in duration from 10 seconds to 60 minutes. Audio standardization and cleaning were executed using the \texttt{librosa} library. Between 1,000 and 5,000 clips underwent rigorous manual auditory review in iterative passes to ensure the fidelity of the final extracted transients.

Empirical evaluation of temporal window lengths was conducted at 250\,ms, 500\,ms, and 1\,s intervals. While the initial ballistic attack transpires over mere milliseconds, the subsequent acoustic decay phase contains the salient features distinguishing a genuine firearm blast from a transient percussive knock. Consequently, a 250\,ms temporal window prematurely truncates this critical reverberant information. As a result, the models presented herein utilize a fixed \textbf{750\,ms} window (16,537 samples at 22,050\,Hz). During live inference, the sliding window operates with a 187.5\,ms hop length (yielding a 75\% overlap), ensuring that a transient occurring on a buffer boundary remains fully encapsulated within a single analytical frame.

Each candidate extraction window was aligned utilizing one of three onset detection cues: spectral flux across STFT bins, the peak absolute sample amplitude, or a constrained moving-average envelope designed to ignore single-sample anomalies. Negative class clips were systematically discarded if their crest factor and attack energy mimicked gunshot profiles. Thus, the negative class strictly comprises ordinary ambient and imposter sounds, preventing the inadvertent inclusion of mislabeled firearms.

It is critical to note that a perfectly balanced 1:1 gunshot-to-ambient distribution artificially inflated the performance of classical classifiers to the 99--100\% range observed in preliminary testing. In a real-world live stream, the distribution of events is heavily skewed (approximately 1:10 or 1:50), as nearly every temporal window consists of silence, speech, or environmental noise. Consequently, all advanced neural network training regimens utilized heavily disproportionate negative class ratios to reflect operational realities.

\section{Hardware Conditioning for Edge Deployment}
\label{sec:conditioning}

The isolated 750\,ms frame requires stringent pre-conditioning prior to neural network ingestion (Stage~2 in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Transitioning from high-fidelity studio environments to real-world edge deployment introduces three primary signal distortions that must be mitigated algorithmically.

First, inexpensive electret microphones operating on standard 3.3\,V or 5\,V voltage rails exhibit inherent DC biasing, resting tens of millivolts off the true zero-crossing. This offset is systematically eradicated by subtracting the sample mean:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{clean}}[n] = y[n] - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} y[i].
    \label{eq:dc_strip}
\end{equation}

Second, standard peak scaling ($y/\max(|y|)$) is mathematically destructive on quiescent windows. A peak value near $0.002$ will inadvertently stretch inherent ADC analog hiss across the entire digital dynamic range. To circumvent this, scaling is applied exclusively via an energy-gated function when the window possesses sufficient root-mean-square (RMS) volume:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{cond}} =
    \begin{cases}
        y / \max(|y|) & \text{if RMS} \ge 0.005, \\
        y & \text{if RMS} < 0.005.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:energy_gate}
\end{equation}

Third, proximate firearm discharges routinely exceed 120\,dB SPL, physically saturating the microphone capsule and resulting in hard digital clipping. To ensure robustness, synthetic hard clipping was aggressively integrated into the training augmentation pipeline, forcing the convolutional filters to recognize the truncated rise-time characteristic of a saturated waveform.

\section{Model Architectures}
\label{sec:models}

Initial baseline evaluations utilizing logistic regression, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Random Forests operating on MFCC summaries achieved 99--100\% accuracy on balanced datasets. However, these models exhibited catastrophic failure during continuous live microphone streaming. This degradation occurs because temporal averaging into a handful of coefficients obliterates the critical microsecond attack signatures of gunshots. Consequently, classical classifiers were deprecated. The five neural network architectures detailed below share the hardware conditioning pipeline outlined in Section~\ref{sec:conditioning}. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the overarching architectural topologies. Exhaustive layer-by-layer listings are omitted for brevity; critical dimensionalities are preserved within the figure.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig_architectures.pdf}
\caption{The four successfully deployed architectural layouts. Note: The Enhanced 1D CNN leverages the identical waveform front-end as the baseline 1D CNN but incorporates a second classification head; however, post-INT8 quantization, it erroneously scores every window as a gunshot and is therefore deprecated.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

The baseline 1D CNN processes the raw 16,537-sample waveform directly. Its initial convolutional kernel is 80 samples wide (approximately $3.6$\,ms at 22,050\,Hz)~\cite{dai2017}, enabling the primary layer to encapsulate rapid acoustic attacks. However, this architecture demonstrates severe overfitting to the training microphones: while hold-out recall achieves 99.7\%, real-world recall on external recordings collapses to 30\%.

The baseline 2D CNN analyzes a 64-band log-mel spectrogram (130 frames, FFT size 512, hop length 128)~\cite{hershey2017}. Preliminary iterations fed unscaled decibel values (ranging from $-80$\,dB to $0$\,dB) into ReLU activation functions. Predictably, ReLU saturated to zero across this negative domain, halting gradient propagation and resulting in a stalled checkpoint that falsely classified all 721 hold-out gunshots as negatives. Subsequent, successful 2D models utilize the normalization scheme:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{norm}} = (S_{\mathrm{dB}} + 80) / 80,
    \label{eq:norm_db}
\end{equation}
which maps the spectrogram matrix into a strict $[0,1]$ domain. Max-pooling operations are deliberately omitted from the final convolutional blocks to prevent the attenuation of high-frequency attack transients.

The Enhanced 2D CNN adopts a similar methodology utilizing a reduced filter width, augmented by MixUp~\cite{zhang2018}, SpecAugment~\cite{park2019}, speed perturbation~\cite{ko2015}, and an auxiliary classification head trained to output an anomaly score. The gunshot classification head dictates the empirical scores reported below. Conversely, the Enhanced 1D CNN applies this identical dual-head architecture directly to the waveform. Unfortunately, INT8 quantization severely saturates the 1D dilated convolutions, causing the model to erroneously classify every temporal window (including absolute silence) as a positive gunshot. It is therefore reported strictly as a failed export pipeline rather than a viable detector. The 2D models, operating on bounded $[0,1]$ spectrograms, quantize flawlessly without suffering this collapse.

The CRNN architecture supplants the static log-mel spectrogram with PCEN~\cite{lostanlen2019,lostanlen2019taslp}, defined as:
\begin{equation}
    P[f,t] = \left( \frac{S[f,t]}{(\epsilon + M[f,t])^{\alpha}} + \delta \right)^{r} - \delta^{r},
    \label{eq:pcen}
\end{equation}
where $M[f,t]$ represents a single-pole IIR temporal smoothing of $S[f,t]$. By configuring $\alpha$ near $0.98$, stationary frequency bands asymptotically settle near unity. Consequently, an arbitrary gain fluctuation $g$ in the physical microphone scales the neural output by merely $g^{0.04}$. A bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)~\cite{cho2014} utilizing 64 units per direction subsequently analyzes the temporal frame sequence, ensuring that sustained tonal frequencies are correctly rejected as non-ballistic. Because PCEN implicitly requires a stable background noise estimation, the initial aggressive silence trimming detailed in Section~\ref{sec:data} is a fundamental prerequisite.

Finally, five TinyML sound event configurations targeting Cortex-M microcontrollers~\cite{cerutti2021} were evaluated as an initial feasibility screen. While standard validation accuracies fluctuated between 98.8\% and 99.7\%, live audio streaming exposed profound brittleness: an MFCC 1D CNN baseline falsely flagged 33 out of 47 ambient silence windows as gunshots. A 2D CNN operating on a mel spectrogram was the first architecture to successfully reject wind and thunder anomalies, operating at a 167\,ms latency. This empirical observation informed the decision to base the architectures in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} entirely on spectrograms.

\section{Experimental Setup and Metrics}
\label{sec:setup}

All empirical neural metrics reported herein utilize strictly exported INT8 quantized models~\cite{david2021}. Inference latency was established over 100 timed iterations executing on the Raspberry Pi~4, with CPU utilization strictly governed at 45--50\% utilizing 2\,GB of allocated RAM~\cite{banbury2021}. The TensorFlow Lite runtime leveraged the exact integer arithmetic path designated for embedded target boards.

The experimental evaluation encompasses three distinct datasets to rigorously assess model generalization. The balanced hold-out dataset consists of 1,442 clips of 750\,ms duration (721 gunshots, 721 ambient/imposter) derived from the primary cleaned corpus. The external dataset contains 75 recordings procured independently of the primary corpus: 20 firearms, 20 imposters, and 35 ambient tracks. The primary validation set features 300 entirely unseen recordings: 100 genuine gunshots, 100 ambient files, and 100 highly challenging percussive imposters (e.g., fireworks, door slams, thunder, drum beats).

Evaluation metrics are defined as follows: \textit{Gunshot recall} is the fraction of genuine firearm files exceeding the detection threshold. \textit{Ambient specificity} represents the fraction of rain, traffic, and speech files correctly rejected below the threshold. \textit{Imposter rejection} indicates the successful suppression of percussive non-firearm events. \textit{Latency} denotes the computational time required to score a single 750\,ms operational window. Given the system's operational hop length of 187.5\,ms, any model exceeding this latency threshold fundamentally violates real-time processing constraints. Unless explicitly stated, the default classification threshold ($\tau$) is defined as $0.50$.

Furthermore, \textit{peak-centered scoring} evaluates a singular 750\,ms acoustic slice perfectly centered upon the maximum waveform amplitude, whereas \textit{sliding scoring} continuously transverses the window across the entire audio file. Naturally, the sliding approach identifies more obscured shots but concomitantly increases the baseline false positive rate.

\subsection{Ensemble Architecture}
The Enhanced 2D CNN, the CRNN-PCEN, and the Baseline 1D CNN were fused into a multi-model ensemble. The logic assumes a positive detection if any constituent model exceeds its respective threshold, subsequently gated by a rapid spectral heuristic check (analyzing energy below 200\,Hz, energy above 4\,kHz, and spectral centroid shifts). Table~\ref{tab:ensemble} and Figure~\ref{fig:cm} report the performance on the 300-clip dataset. This ensemble approach successfully elevates recall from 67\% to 77\% (capturing 77 out of 100 true gunshots). False positives remain a challenge: 44 out of 100 hard imposters and 20 out of 100 ambient files triggered false alerts. The total ensemble memory footprint is approximately 715\,KB, requiring a combined 17\,ms execution time, which remains well within the strict operational hop boundary.

The performance augmentation is driven by complementary failure modes rather than redundant feature extraction. For instance, on the file \texttt{127724.wav}, the Enhanced 2D CNN generates a low 21.1\% confidence, whereas the CRNN correctly identifies the event with 99.3\% confidence. Conversely, on \texttt{170167.wav}, the CRNN fails entirely (0\% confidence) while the Enhanced 2D CNN triggers accurately at 99.6\%. Table~\ref{tab:paradigm} contextualizes these operational tradeoffs. For an initial, resource-constrained sensor node, the standalone Enhanced 2D model offers the optimal balance of speed and precision. However, if the central gateway is capable of algorithmically filtering higher false-positive rates, the three-model ensemble provides the superior recall required for maximum public safety.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study conclusively demonstrates that while classical machine learning classifiers achieve near-perfect accuracy on balanced hold-out datasets, they fail catastrophically during live deployment because temporal averaging irreversibly destroys the microsecond shockwave signatures intrinsic to firearms. The architectural transition from 1D MFCC classifiers to 2D spectrogram-based CNNs yielded substantial improvements in the rejection of ambient noise and percussive imposters. Evaluated against 300 highly complex unseen recordings, the proposed Enhanced 2D CNN achieved 67\% sliding gunshot recall and 99\% peak ambient specificity, executing in a remarkable 0.09\,ms while occupying only 124\,KB of flash memory. Furthermore, the implementation of a PCEN-CRNN architecture demonstrated unparalleled resilience, maintaining stable recall under severe simulated microphone distortion with zero algorithmic degradation, thereby validating PCEN as a superior domain-shift normalizer for acoustic edge applications. The Enhanced 1D CNN was deemed fundamentally non-deployable following INT8 conversion due to catastrophic weight saturation within its dilated convolutional filters.

A critical physical limitation was also identified during empirical testing: a solitary microphone is inherently incapable of distinguishing between aerial mortar fireworks and genuine gunfire, as both events generate virtually identical shockwave rise-times and reverberation profiles in the time domain. Resolving this acoustic ambiguity necessitates the future integration of spatial multi-microphone arrays.

The subsequent phases of this research will prioritize the following hardware integration tasks:

\textbf{1) Physical Edge Deployment.} Systematically deploy the Enhanced 2D CNN INT8 quantized model onto a Raspberry Pi~4 architecture to validate deterministic edge timing, followed by a direct port of the inference engine to the highly constrained Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense (utilizing the on-board ST MP34DT05 PDM microphone).

\textbf{2) Duty-Cycled Deep Sleep and Minimalist Telemetry.} The edge node will maintain a deep sleep state (drawing approximately 5\,$\mu$A) until a coarse analog energy threshold triggers a hardware interrupt~\cite{cerutti2021}. A single inference cycle is subsequently executed before returning to sleep. Upon a positive threshold breach, the node will transmit a minimalist packet structure comprising: node ID (1~byte), timestamp (4~bytes), detection flag (1~bit), and confidence score (1~byte). This protocol restricts radio transmission time to under 1.5\,ms per trigger event. Negative inferences trigger no transmission, ensuring that raw audio is strictly confined to the local node to guarantee absolute privacy.

\textbf{3) Multi-Node Spatial Consensus.} To mitigate false positives generated by localized percussive anomalies (e.g., a proximal hand clap), the central gateway will elevate a system-wide alert exclusively when a spatial consensus of $K \ge 3$ geographically distributed nodes transmit concurrent detection flags within a highly constrained acoustic propagation window (approximately 100\,ms for a 34\,m sensor spacing). This topological approach effectively filters localized imposters without compromising network bandwidth or streaming raw audio.

\textbf{4) Self-Healing Network Diagnostics and Dashboard Integration.} Embedded nodes will autonomously transmit periodic diagnostic telemetry encompassing battery degradation, sensor baseline drift, and radio link integrity. A centralized dashboard deployed at the gateway will continuously aggregate this telemetry and log every positive trigger, establishing a comprehensive, immutable audit trail for post-incident forensic analysis.
