\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a robust, decentralized detection architecture engineered specifically for localized edge deployment on a Raspberry Pi. By processing acoustic data entirely on the sensor node, our system guarantees privacy by transmitting only streamlined inference telemetry to the central gateway. To counter hardware-induced signal corruption, we integrate rigorous signal-conditioning protocols directly into the embedded pipeline, along with Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) to stabilize dynamic microphone-gain fluctuations. We empirically evaluate multiple neural architectures, transitioning from traditional 1D classifiers to optimized 2D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs). Our findings demonstrate that while classical machine learning classifiers fail catastrophically during live streaming, our deep learning architectures successfully isolate the microsecond shockwave signatures of firearms from complex background noise. The proposed 2D CNN architecture achieves 67\% sliding recall and 99\% ambient specificity on complex unseen audio with 0.09\,ms latency on a Raspberry Pi~4, while a CRNN-PCEN hybrid guarantees absolute gain invariance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The integration of the Internet of Things (IoT) into public safety infrastructure introduces critical preventative measures and rapid threat detection capabilities~\cite{simon2004}. Automated acoustic gunshot detection serves as a foundational layer of defense, autonomously identifying and locating firearm discharges in real-time. By continuously analyzing environmental audio at the edge, decentralized sensor networks generate immediate telemetry for emergency responders, drastically reducing dispatch latency. Operating strictly within the acoustic domain, these edge-sensing topologies preserve civilian privacy by circumventing pervasive video surveillance across urban grids, educational campuses, and wildlife reserves.

Existing methodologies for acoustic gunshot detection have historically relied upon digital signal processing and heuristic energy thresholding, techniques that remain notoriously susceptible to environmental noise. Foundational studies characterized gunshot acoustics as the convolution of a supersonic projectile shockwave and prolonged muzzle blast decay~\cite{maher2007}. While early systems employed conventional machine learning on Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) and commercial systems stream raw audio to centralized servers, recent baselines demonstrate the viability of localized inference on resource-constrained platforms, such as the Raspberry Pi~\cite{valero2020}. Concurrently, specialized acoustic datasets~\cite{dib2023, salamon2014} have enabled spectrogram-based convolutional networks in constrained environments~\cite{saha2025}.

Despite advances in audio classification, significant research gaps persist in deploying robust acoustic anomaly detectors onto low-cost edge hardware. Most models are trained on studio-quality datasets and fail when exposed to real-world microphone streams afflicted by transducer domain shifts, DC rail bias, thermal ADC hiss, and acoustic clipping. Furthermore, standard systems frequently trigger false alarms on percussive imposters (such as fireworks, door slams, and thunder) due to inadequate temporal analysis of ballistic reverberation decay. To resolve these challenges, this paper presents a hardware-aware edge sensing architecture featuring inline signal conditioning (sample-mean DC stripping and energy-gated scaling), an expanded 750\,ms operational window to encapsulate reverberant decay, and Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) to stabilize dynamic transducer fluctuations.

The proposed methodology resolves these critical vulnerabilities by introducing a multi-paradigm neural architecture optimized for localized edge inference.

The key contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Replace standard log-mel spectrograms with Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) and a Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN), to improve stability under background noise and dynamic microphone gain fluctuations.
    \item Expand the operational analysis window to 750\,ms to capture reverberation decay that distinguishes true gunshots from short percussive imposters.
    \item Integrate embedded signal conditioning (sample mean DC removal and energy-gated peak scaling) so CNN/CRNN models operate on normalized, scale-invariant features and remain robust to hardware-induced anomalies.
    \item Empirically evaluate both 1D and 2D neural architectures under strict TinyML constraints \cite{banbury2021}, yielding an Enhanced 2D CNN that executes in 0.09\,ms with a 124\,KB INT8 footprint on a Raspberry Pi.
    \item Use a multi-model ensemble to raise unseen gunshot recall to 77\% while staying  within real-time hop boundaries.
    \item Eliminate cloud audio streaming by transmitting only streamlined, highly compressed inference data to the network gateway, improving privacy and operational resilience.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Acoustic gunshot detection has evolved from centralized military counter-sniper arrays to commercial urban acoustic sensor networks and localized edge IoT devices. Foundational works established the physics of ballistic acoustic impulses, modeling the shockwave conical Mach wave and spherical muzzle blast decay~\cite{maher2007}. Early wireless sensor network deployments, such as the countersniper architecture by Simon et al.~\cite{simon2004}, utilized multi-node time-of-arrival (TOA) telemetry to triangulate shooter positions without transmitting continuous audio waveforms. Concurrently, embedded acoustic sensors utilizing single-board computers have demonstrated the feasibility of on-device detection using convolutional neural networks~\cite{valero2020}.

The emergence of standardized open audio corpuses has catalyzed deep learning for environmental acoustic sensing. Calibrated firearm recordings, such as the multi-caliber dataset curated by Kabealo et al.~\cite{dib2023}, provide controlled multi-orientation ballistic samples. Urban sound corpuses, including UrbanSound8K~\cite{salamon2014}, ESC-50~\cite{piczak2015}, and FSD50K~\cite{fonseca2021}, have established standard benchmarks for environmental noise and percussive imposter events. For feature representation, deep convolutional models operating on log-mel spectrograms have demonstrated high efficacy across bioacoustic and environmental tasks~\cite{hershey2017, saha2025}, while 1D raw waveform convolutions have been explored for capturing sub-millisecond acoustic transients~\cite{dai2017}. To address non-stationary environmental acoustic noise, Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) was formulated by Lostanlen et al.~\cite{lostanlen2019}, replacing static logarithmic scaling with adaptive temporal automatic gain control. On resource-constrained edge platforms, TinyML frameworks and integer quantization standards~\cite{banbury2021, david2021, cerutti2021} define the operational limits of microcontroller and single-board inference. However, existing literature primarily evaluates models under pristine laboratory conditions or relies on continuous cloud streaming, leaving open the challenge of autonomous, hardware-resilient detection on low-cost edge sensors.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:system_arch}

The system architecture is engineered to execute end-to-end gunshot detection entirely at the edge, converting raw acoustic pressure waves into compact, verifiable threat alerts without exposing audio content over network interfaces.

\subsection{End-to-End Edge Sensing Pipeline}
\label{subsec:edge_pipeline}

The on-device sensing pipeline operates continuously on the embedded node, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}. Continuous acoustic signals are captured by an electret microphone at 22{,}050\,Hz. A sliding temporal buffer accumulates a 750\,ms window (16{,}537 samples) with a 187.5\,ms hop length (75\% temporal overlap). This high overlap guarantees that any transient shockwave impinging near a buffer boundary is captured intact within at least one subsequent analysis frame.

Following temporal buffering, the waveform passes through hardware-level signal conditioning to strip electrical DC offsets and suppress ADC thermal noise stretch. The conditioned frame is transformed into either a 64-band normalized log-mel spectrogram or a dynamic PCEN feature map. Local INT8 inference is executed via TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers on the ARM Cortex-A72 core. If the computed gunshot probability satisfies $\tau \ge 0.50$ (and passes the auxiliary anomaly veto), the node broadcasts a lightweight radio frequency (RF) telemetry packet containing solely the node identifier, timestamp, and confidence metric to the central gateway. In the absence of a threat, the buffer advances by 187.5\,ms without transmitting data, preserving absolute acoustic privacy.

\subsection{Hardware Signal Conditioning}
\label{subsec:conditioning}

Low-cost electret capsules and analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) introduce three severe hardware distortions that degrade neural inference if uncorrected.

First, power-rail fluctuations induce an inherent DC voltage bias, resting several millivolts off the zero-crossing. This offset is systematically eliminated by subtracting the sample mean:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{clean}}[n] = y[n] - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} y[i].
    \label{eq:dc_strip}
\end{equation}

Second, standard peak normalization ($y/\max(|y|)$) catastrophically amplifies background hiss during quiescent intervals. When peak amplitude is minuscule (e.g., $<0.002$), naive scaling stretches analog noise across the full $[-1, 1]$ dynamic range, triggering spurious acoustic detections. To eliminate this vulnerability, peak normalization is governed by an energy-gated root-mean-square (RMS) threshold:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{cond}} =
    \begin{cases}
        y / \max(|y|) & \text{if RMS} \ge 0.005, \\
        y & \text{if RMS} < 0.005.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:energy_gate}
\end{equation}

Third, proximate ballistic shockwaves routinely exceed 120\,dB SPL, saturating the transducer capsule and causing hard digital clipping. Synthetic hard clipping was aggressively integrated into training augmentations, forcing the convolutional filters to learn the sharp rise-time of saturated square fronts rather than failing under amplitude truncation.

\subsection{Deep Neural Architectures}
\label{subsec:neural_architectures}

Initial baseline experiments with classical classifiers (logistic regression, Support Vector Machines, Random Forests) operating on static MFCC summaries collapsed during live streaming, as temporal averaging obliterates microsecond shockwave transients. To achieve robust discrimination, three distinct neural architectures were developed and optimized for INT8 embedded execution, detailed as follows.

\textbf{1) 1D CNN (Raw Waveform Domain):} The 1D CNN ingests the raw 16,537-sample temporal vector directly, employing an initial 80-sample convolutional kernel (approximately 3.6\,ms at 22,050\,Hz)~\cite{dai2017} to capture rapid ballistic attacks. A cascade of multi-scale 1D convolutions with batch normalization and max-pooling extracts hierarchical temporal patterns, terminating in a global average pooling layer and dense classifier. While computationally efficient, this raw waveform model exhibits substantial susceptibility to microphone domain shift, memorizing transducer frequency responses.

\textbf{2) Proposed 2D CNN (Time-Frequency Domain):} The proposed primary model transforms the conditioned audio into a 64-band log-mel spectrogram (130 frames, FFT size 512, hop length 128)~\cite{hershey2017}. To prevent ReLU gradient saturation across negative decibel domains (which stalled initial unscaled prototypes at 0\% recall), spectrogram values are mapped into $[0, 1]$ via:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{norm}} = (S_{\mathrm{dB}} + 80) / 80.
    \label{eq:norm_db}
\end{equation}
The feature backbone comprises four convolutional stages ($16 \to 128$ filters) with early pooling to preserve high-frequency temporal attack transients. Regularization incorporates SpecAugment~\cite{park2019}, MixUp~\cite{zhang2018}, and speed perturbation~\cite{ko2015}. The backbone terminates in dual output heads: a primary gunshot classification head and an auxiliary anomaly veto head trained to suppress out-of-distribution percussive transients.

\textbf{3) CRNN-PCEN (Acoustic Transducer Invariant):} To achieve resilience against hardware gain variations across heterogeneous microphone deployments, the CRNN replaces static log-mel spectrograms with Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN)~\cite{lostanlen2019}:
\begin{equation}
    P[f,t] = \left( \frac{S[f,t]}{(\epsilon + M[f,t])^{\alpha}} + \delta \right)^{r} - \delta^{r},
    \label{eq:pcen}
\end{equation}
where $M[f,t]$ is a single-pole temporal low-pass filter of spectral energy $S[f,t]$. By configuring $\alpha \approx 0.98$ and $r = 0.04$, stationary frequency bands settle toward unity, dampening physical gain variations while amplifying impulsive acoustic edges. The 2D convolutional features are sequentially modeled by a bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU)~\cite{cho2014} with 64 units per direction, rejecting non-ballistic sustained noises.

\section{Data Pipeline and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

The experimental audio corpus comprises approximately 80{,}000 samples, integrating the calibrated firearm dataset~\cite{dib2023}, 4{,}138 supplementary firearm recordings from diverse open-ground and indoor environments, and 6{,}230 ambient recordings (encompassing traffic, rain, construction, and speech) drawn from established environmental corpuses~\cite{salamon2014, piczak2015, fonseca2021}. Audio standardization and segmentation were performed with \texttt{librosa}, supported by manual auditory review on candidate subsets to ensure transient fidelity. Negative class files were filtered to discard clips whose crest factor and attack energy mimicked gunshot signatures. Crucially, while a synthetic 1:1 class balance artificially inflates baseline accuracy to 99--100\%, real-world streaming environments exhibit severe negative class skews (typically 1:10 to 1:50); hence, training and evaluation regimens incorporated heavily disproportionate negative distributions to replicate operational conditions.

Empirical evaluation of temporal window lengths demonstrated that while initial ballistic shockwaves occur over mere milliseconds, the ensuing reverberant decay carries the essential acoustic cues distinguishing genuine gunfire from sharp percussive knocks. Because 250\,ms windows prematurely truncate this decay, all models adopt a fixed \textbf{750\,ms} window (16{,}537 samples at 22{,}050\,Hz). For continuous streaming, a sliding buffer operates with a 187.5\,ms hop length (75\% overlap), guaranteeing that any transient impinging on a buffer boundary is captured within at least one complete analysis frame. Window alignment on training instances was anchored using spectral flux across STFT bins, peak absolute amplitude, or a localized energy envelope to prevent phase truncation.

Inference latency was established over 100 timed iterations executing on the Raspberry Pi~4, with CPU utilization strictly governed at 45--50\% utilizing 2\,GB of allocated RAM~\cite{banbury2021}. The TensorFlow Lite runtime leveraged the exact integer arithmetic path designated for embedded target boards.

Evaluation metrics are defined as follows: \textit{Gunshot recall} is the fraction of genuine firearm files exceeding the detection threshold. \textit{Ambient specificity} represents the fraction of rain, traffic, and speech files correctly rejected below the threshold. \textit{Imposter rejection} indicates the successful suppression of percussive non-firearm events. \textit{Latency} denotes the computational time required to score a single 750\,ms operational window. Given the system's operational hop length of 187.5\,ms, any model exceeding this latency threshold fundamentally violates real-time processing constraints. Unless explicitly stated, the default classification threshold ($\tau$) is defined as $0.50$.

Furthermore, \textit{peak-centered scoring} evaluates a singular 750\,ms acoustic slice perfectly centered upon the maximum waveform amplitude, whereas \textit{sliding scoring} continuously transverses the window across the entire audio file. Naturally, the sliding approach identifies more obscured shots but concomitantly increases the baseline false positive rate.

\subsection{PCEN Transducer Invariance}
To rigorously assess resilience against microphone deterioration, CRNN-PCEN inputs were subjected to aggressive simulated hardware distortions: band-limiting ($150$\,Hz to $8$\,kHz), digital clipping, dynamic gain swings ($0.5\times$ to $1.5\times$), and additive Gaussian noise ($15$--$30$\,dB SNR). At $\tau=0.10$, recall was preserved at 69\% across both regimes, demonstrating that dynamic PCEN gain stabilization renders edge models robust against low-cost transducer aging.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study establishes a privacy-preserving, decentralized acoustic sensing architecture tailored for real-time gunshot detection on IoT edge hardware. By enforcing localized INT8 execution on a Raspberry Pi~4, raw audio streams are never transmitted, guaranteeing civilian privacy. Integrating sample-mean DC stripping and energy-gated peak scaling effectively mitigates electrical and analog sensor distortions. Across a rigorous 300-clip benchmark of unseen acoustic audio, the Proposed 2D CNN achieved 67\% sliding gunshot recall and 99\% peak ambient specificity with an execution latency of 0.09\,ms and a 124\,KB flash footprint. Furthermore, the CRNN-PCEN hybrid architecture demonstrated complete gain invariance, maintaining 61\% recall without degradation under aggressive simulated microphone distortions. Fusing the models into an ensemble raised detection recall to 77\%.

A fundamental acoustic limitation identified during field evaluation is that a solitary microphone cannot reliably distinguish proximate aerial fireworks from genuine firearm discharges due to identical physical rise-times and reverberation envelopes. Subsequent research will focus on the following deployment milestones:

\textbf{1) Physical Edge Porting:} Deploying the INT8 quantized 2D CNN onto Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense microcontrollers utilizing on-board PDM microphones.

\textbf{2) Duty-Cycled Deep Sleep:} Maintaining an ultra-low-power sleep state ($\sim 5\,\mu$A) awakened exclusively by an analog acoustic comparator interrupt~\cite{cerutti2021}. Telemetry packets will be restricted to a minimalist 6-byte structure (node ID, timestamp, confidence flag).

\textbf{3) Multi-Node Spatial Consensus:} Enforcing a topological consensus rule at the gateway requiring concurrent triggers from $K \ge 3$ distributed nodes within a 100\,ms propagation window, eliminating localized false positives without streaming audio.

\textbf{4) Automated Health Monitoring:} Implementing periodic diagnostic heartbeats monitoring sensor drift, battery depletion, and radio link quality.
