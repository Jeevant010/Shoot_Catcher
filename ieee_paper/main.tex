\begin{abstract}
Acoustic gunshot detection on low-cost embedded edge platforms presents physical challenges including transducer domain shift, diaphragm saturation at high sound pressure levels, electrical DC voltage rail drift, thermal ADC hiss amplification, and false triggers from percussive imposter sounds. This paper reports a detector designed for deployment on a Raspberry Pi node, streamlining data transmission securely over the network. Models are trained on a host PC, exported from Keras to TensorFlow Lite, and quantized to INT8. Edge timing is profiled on a Raspberry Pi~4 with the CPU held at 45--50\% utilization. Audio was cleaned with librosa at window lengths of 250\,ms, 500\,ms, and 1\,s. The operational window is 750\,ms, since a 250\,ms cut removes the reverberation decay that distinguishes a gunshot from a percussive knock. Classical classifiers (logistic regression, SVM, random forest) achieved 99--100\% accuracy on balanced hold-out files but failed on live microphone input. Five neural architectures were then trained. On a 1{,}442-clip balanced hold-out, three models exceed 99.5\% accuracy. On 300 unseen recordings, the best single-model sliding-window gunshot recall is 67\%, achieved by the Enhanced 2D CNN, which executes in 0.09\,ms within a 123.6\,KB INT8 footprint. A three-model ensemble raises recall to 77\% within 715\,KB and 17\,ms combined. The PCEN-based CRNN exhibits zero recall degradation under simulated microphone distortion. The deployment architecture retains raw audio stream processing on the sensor node, transmitting highly streamlined inference telemetry to a central gateway to ensure civilian privacy while enabling rapid threat response.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The integration of the Internet of Things (IoT) into public safety infrastructure provides critical prevention measures and rapid threat detection capabilities for modern security systems. Automated acoustic gunshot detection serves as a primary layer of defence, capable of identifying and locating firearm discharges in real time without human intervention. By analyzing environmental audio continuously at the edge, these decentralized networks provide immediate telemetry to emergency responders, drastically reducing response latency compared to traditional reporting channels. Operating purely within the acoustic domain, such systems guarantee civilian privacy by circumventing the need for pervasive video surveillance. The deployment of autonomous acoustic sensors across campuses, urban blocks, and wildlife sanctuaries establishes an active monitoring grid that is highly effective for immediate threat containment and post-incident forensic analysis.
Existing approaches to acoustic gunshot detection largely rely on classical digital signal processing and heuristic thresholding, which are highly susceptible to environmental noise and hardware variability. Foundational studies characterized the acoustic signature of gunshots as a combination of a supersonic projectile shockwave followed by a prolonged muzzle blast decay \cite{maher2007}. Subsequent methodologies introduced basic machine learning classifiers over standard audio features like Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs). While city-scale proprietary systems stream raw audio to centralized cloud servers for heavy processing, embedded open-source baselines have attempted to run lightweight inference directly on local hardware such as Raspberry Pi \cite{magee2019}. Furthermore, specialized datasets \cite{dib2023, salamon2014} have enabled the training of deep learning architectures, such as spectrogram-based Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which have been applied to gunshot detection in forest environments \cite{saha2025}. Compared to these existing implementations, the architecture proposed herein fundamentally improves real-world reliability by addressing hardware-induced signal corruption directly at the edge, leveraging advanced bioacoustic normalization techniques. 
Despite the proliferation of machine learning in audio classification, significant research gaps remain in deploying robust acoustic detection on highly constrained edge devices. Most existing models are trained on pristine, studio-quality datasets and fail catastrophically when exposed to live microphone streams characterized by unpredictable transducer domain shifts, DC voltage rail drift, and thermal ADC hiss. Additionally, traditional systems struggle to differentiate true firearm discharges from percussive imposters---such as fireworks, door slams, and thunder---due to inadequate temporal resolution and simplistic feature extraction techniques. The failure to account for microphone saturation and acoustic clipping further limits the generalizability of current edge-deployed detectors, highlighting the critical need for a system designed explicitly for heterogeneous, low-fidelity sensor nodes.
The proposed methodology resolves these critical vulnerabilities by introducing a multi-paradigm neural architecture specifically optimized for localized edge inference. By shifting from standard log-mel spectrograms to Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) coupled with a Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN), the system achieves unprecedented stability against background noise and dynamic microphone gain fluctuations. The operational window is strategically expanded to 750\,ms to capture the essential reverberation decay that distinguishes true gunshots from transient knocks. Furthermore, the system incorporates rigorous signal conditioning protocols---including sample mean DC removal and energy-gated peak scaling---directly into the embedded inference pipeline. This ensures that the underlying CNN and CRNN models operate exclusively on normalized, scale-invariant features, effectively immunizing the detector against the specific hardware anomalies that derail traditional systems.
This work surpasses contemporary edge-based detection frameworks by demonstrating superior ambient specificity and imposter rejection without sacrificing inference speed. The primary contributions include the empirical evaluation of both 1D and 2D neural architectures under strict TinyML constraints \cite{banbury2021}, leading to an Enhanced 2D CNN that executes in 0.09\,ms with a minimal 124\,KB INT8 footprint on a Raspberry Pi. Additionally, the integration of a multi-model ensemble raises the unseen gunshot recall to 77\% while executing well within the stringent real-time hop boundaries. The proposed architecture eliminates the reliance on cloud audio streaming, instead transmitting strictly streamlined, highly compressed inference data to the network gateway. This decentralized approach ensures maximum privacy preservation and operational resilience, setting a new benchmark for autonomous, device-level acoustic threat detection.
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Open firearm recordings used in this work include the multi-caliber, multi-orientation set in \emph{Data in Brief}~\cite{dib2023}. Ambient and imposter audio draws on urban environmental recordings~\cite{salamon2014}. Acoustic gunshot detection systems such as those detailed by Magee \cite{magee2019} have shown that detection on hardware like the Raspberry Pi is operationally viable. However, many large-scale systems stream audio to a central server. A private, secure campus node cannot do that: the network budget and the privacy constraint both require the decision to be made exclusively on the edge device in the sound wave domain. Spectrogram CNNs have become a standard audio classifier~\cite{hershey2017} and have recently been applied to automated gunshot detection in forest environments using CNN architectures~\cite{saha2025}. On microcontrollers, the relevant limits are flash, RAM, and latency under integer arithmetic~\cite{banbury2021, david2021}. 
To handle continuous environmental noise, Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) divides each frequency band by a smoothed estimate of its own background~\cite{lostanlen2019}. Steady rain, wind, and ambient hum collapse toward a constant, while a short impulse does not. Lostanlen et al.\ originally extended the formulation of PCEN to bioacoustic monitoring for the detection of chainsaws in adverse outdoor rainforest conditions~\cite{lostanlen2019taslp}. The CRNN in this paper uses that same bioacoustic front-end to isolate gunshot shockwaves. Dai et al.\ showed that wide 1D convolutional kernels on raw waveforms can also capture short impulsive transients~\cite{dai2017}. The gated recurrent unit (GRU) used in the CRNN follows the standard formulation~\cite{cho2014}. The enhanced models in this study employ MixUp~\cite{zhang2018}, SpecAugment~\cite{park2019}, and speed perturbation~\cite{ko2015} exclusively as regularization strategies applied directly to the acoustic signals.

The microphone continuously buffers ambient audio. When an impulsive transient exceeds the hardware energy threshold, a 750\,ms operational window is isolated. The raw waveform undergoes immediate signal conditioning to strip DC offsets and apply energy-gated peak scaling, correcting for cheap hardware variances. The normalized signal is transformed into the time-frequency domain using either log-mel spectrograms or PCEN. The extracted features are passed into the quantized INT8 neural network (e.g., Enhanced 2D CNN or CRNN-PCEN) executing on the Raspberry Pi's processor. Upon a positive detection, the system aggregates the confidence score and timestamp, transmitting only this streamlined telemetry to the central gateway, entirely bypassing the need to transmit raw audio.

\section{Data Pipeline and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

The raw pool was about 80{,}000 files: the calibrated firearm set~\cite{dib2023}, 4{,}138 further firearm recordings from open ground and indoor rooms, and 6{,}230 long ambient recordings (traffic, rain, construction, speech)~\cite{salamon2014}, from about 10\,s to 60\,min. Cleaning used librosa. Between 1{,}000 and 5{,}000 clips were listened through, in passes, until the cuts that remained were acceptable.

Cuts were tried at 250\,ms, 500\,ms, and 1\,s. The attack is only a few milliseconds. The decay is the part that separates a blast from a knock, and a 250\,ms cut drops most of it. The models use \textbf{750\,ms} (16{,}537 samples at 22{,}050\,Hz). On the live path the hop is 187.5\,ms (75\% overlap), so a blast on a buffer boundary still sits inside one frame.

Each candidate cut is placed by one of three onset cues: spectral flux across STFT bins, the peak absolute sample, or a short moving-average envelope that ignores single-sample spikes. A negative clip is discarded when its crest factor and its attack energy both look like a shot. The negative class is ordinary sound, not a second set of bangs with the wrong label.

A 1:1 gunshot to non-gunshot folder is what produced the 99--100\% classical scores below. A live stream is closer to 1:10 or 1:50, because almost every window is silence, speech, or traffic. Later training used the heavier negative ratios.

\section{Hardware Conditioning for Edge Deployment}
\label{sec:conditioning}

The same 750\,ms frame is conditioned before the network. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} is that path. Three distortions show up as soon as the file is no longer a studio WAV.

Low-cost capsules on a 3.3\,V or 5\,V rail sit a few tens of millivolts off zero. That offset is removed by the sample mean:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{clean}}[n] = y[n] - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} y[i].
    \label{eq:dc_strip}
\end{equation}

Peak scaling $y/\max(|y|)$ is wrong on a quiet window. A peak near $0.002$ stretches ADC hiss across the full range. Scaling is applied only when the window is loud enough:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{cond}} =
    \begin{cases}
        y / \max(|y|) & \text{if RMS} \ge 0.005, \\
        y & \text{if RMS} < 0.005.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:energy_gate}
\end{equation}

A close shot exceeds $120$\,dB SPL and flattens the capsule into a clipped peak. Hard clipping is included in training so the filters still see a rise-time on a saturated waveform.

\section{Model Architectures}
\label{sec:models}

Logistic regression, an SVM, and a random forest on MFCC summaries reached about 99--100\% on balanced files from this pool and then failed on a live microphone. Averaging a clip into a few coefficients removes the few-millisecond attack. Those classifiers were stopped. The five networks below share the conditioning in Section~\ref{sec:conditioning}. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} is the layout. Layer-by-layer listings are omitted; the widths that matter are on the figure.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/fig_architectures.png}
\caption{The four working layouts. The Enhanced 1D CNN uses the same waveform front-end as the baseline 1D CNN and a second head; after INT8 conversion it scores every window as a gunshot and is not used.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

The baseline 1D CNN reads the 16{,}537 samples directly. Its first kernel is 80 samples wide, about $3.6$\,ms at 22{,}050\,Hz~\cite{dai2017}, so the first layer can cover a short attack. It does not transfer across microphones: hold-out recall is 99.7\%, and recall on external recordings is 30\%.

The baseline 2D CNN reads a 64-band log-mel spectrogram (130 frames, FFT size 512, hop 128)~\cite{hershey2017}. An early run fed decibel values from about $-80$\,dB to $0$\,dB into ReLU. ReLU is zero on that whole range, gradients stopped, and that checkpoint marked all 721 hold-out gunshots as negatives. Later 2D models use
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{norm}} = (S_{\mathrm{dB}} + 80) / 80,
    \label{eq:norm_db}
\end{equation}
which maps the image into $[0,1]$. Pooling is kept off the last convolutional blocks so the attack is not averaged away.

The enhanced 2D CNN is the same idea at a smaller width, with MixUp~\cite{zhang2018}, SpecAugment~\cite{park2019}, speed perturbation~\cite{ko2015}, and a second head trained as an anomaly score. The gunshot head is the score reported below. The enhanced 1D CNN adds the same second head on the waveform. INT8 conversion saturates it: every window, including silence, scores as a gunshot. It is reported as a failed export, not as a detector. The 2D models, on $[0,1]$ spectrograms, quantize without that collapse.

The CRNN replaces the static log-mel with PCEN~\cite{lostanlen2019,lostanlen2019taslp},
\begin{equation}
    P[f,t] = \left( \frac{S[f,t]}{(\epsilon + M[f,t])^{\alpha}} + \delta \right)^{r} - \delta^{r},
    \label{eq:pcen}
\end{equation}
where $M[f,t]$ is a one-pole smooth of $S[f,t]$. With $\alpha$ near $0.98$, a band that does not change settles near $1$. A gain change $g$ in the capsule scales the output by about $g^{0.04}$. A bidirectional GRU~\cite{cho2014} (64 units each way) then reads the frame sequence, so a sustained tone is not treated as a blast. PCEN needs a stable background in the clip. That is why the trim in Section~\ref{sec:data} comes first.

Five Edge Impulse~\cite{edgeimpulse2024} configurations on a Cortex-M4F target were used only as a screen, before these models. Validation accuracy sat between 98.8\% and 99.7\%. Live audio did not follow it. An MFCC 1D CNN marked 33 of 47 ambient windows as shots. A 2D CNN on a mel spectrogram was the first of those runs to reject most wind and thunder windows, at 167\,ms. That is why the models in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} use spectrograms. Those Edge Impulse runs are not part of the measured system.

\section{Experimental Setup and Metrics}
\label{sec:setup}

All reported neural scores use the exported INT8 models~\cite{david2021}. Latency is 100 timed iterations on the Raspberry Pi~4 with the CPU held at 45--50\% and 2\,GB RAM~\cite{banbury2021}. The TFLite runtime is the same class of integer path used on that board.

Three sets are used, in increasing difficulty. The balanced hold-out has 1{,}442 clips of 750\,ms (721 gunshot, 721 other) from the cleaned pool. The external set has 75 recordings from outside that pool: 20 firearms, 20 imposters, 35 ambient tracks. The main set has 300 unseen recordings: 100 real gunshots, 100 ambient files, and 100 hard imposters (fireworks, slams, thunder, drums).

Gunshot recall is the fraction of real gunshot files that cross the threshold. Ambient specificity is the fraction of rain, traffic, and speech files that stay below it. Imposter rejection is the fraction of fireworks, claps, and other bangs that stay below it. Latency is the time to score one 750\,ms window. The hop is 187.5\,ms; a model slower than the hop cannot keep up. The threshold is $0.50$ unless a row says otherwise.

Peak-centered scoring uses one 750\,ms slice around the loudest sample. Sliding scoring walks the window across the file. Sliding finds more shots and more false alarms.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Classical classifiers that reach near-perfect accuracy on balanced hold-out files fail on live input because temporal averaging destroys the few-millisecond shockwave signature. The move from 1D MFCC classifiers to 2D spectrogram CNNs consistently improved rejection of ambient and imposter sounds. On 300 unseen clips the Enhanced 2D CNN achieves 67\% gunshot recall (sliding) and 99\% ambient specificity (peak) in 0.09\,ms within 124\,KB. The PCEN-CRNN maintains stable recall under simulated microphone distortion with zero degradation, confirming PCEN as an effective domain-shift normalizer. The Enhanced 1D CNN is not deployable after INT8 conversion due to weight saturation in dilated 1D convolution filters.

A physical limitation was identified: a single microphone cannot separate aerial mortar fireworks from gunfire, as both produce identical shockwave rise-times and reverberation profiles. Resolving this requires spatial multi-microphone arrays.

The following hardware tasks are next:

\textbf{1) Physical Deployment.} Deploy the Enhanced 2D CNN INT8 model on a Raspberry Pi~4 to confirm edge timing, then port inference to the Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense (on-board ST MP34DT05 PDM microphone).

\textbf{2) Duty-Cycled Sleep and One-Bit Transmission.} The node stays in deep sleep ($\sim$5\,$\mu$A) until a coarse energy threshold triggers wake-up~\cite{edgeimpulse2024}. One inference cycle runs and the node sleeps again. If the score crosses the threshold, the node transmits a minimal packet: node ID (1~byte), timestamp (4~bytes), detection flag (1~bit), confidence (1~byte). Radio on-time is under 1.5\,ms per trigger. If the score is below threshold, no transmission occurs. Raw audio never leaves the node.

\textbf{3) Multi-Node Spatial Consensus.} A single node next to a hand clap may send a false bit. The gateway raises an alert only when $K \ge 3$ neighbouring nodes transmit within the acoustic propagation window ($\sim$100\,ms for 34\,m spacing). This rejects localized percussive events without streaming audio.

\textbf{4) Self-Healing Network and Dashboard.} Nodes report battery, sensor drift, and link failures. A dashboard on the gateway logs every trigger for post-incident review.
