\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a robust, decentralized detection architecture engineered specifically for localized edge deployment on a Raspberry Pi. By processing acoustic data entirely on the sensor node, our system guarantees privacy by transmitting only streamlined inference telemetry to the central gateway. To counter hardware-induced signal corruption, we integrate rigorous signal-conditioning protocols directly into the embedded pipeline, along with Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) to stabilize dynamic microphone-gain fluctuations. We empirically evaluate multiple neural architectures, transitioning from traditional 1D classifiers to optimized 2D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs). Our findings demonstrate that while classical machine learning classifiers fail catastrophically during live streaming, our enhanced deep learning ensemble successfully isolates the microsecond shockwave signatures of firearms from complex background noise. The proposed architecture maintains strict real-time inference latency and exceptional resilience against transducer variability, establishing a highly secure, autonomous benchmark for IoT-based public safety networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The integration of the Internet of Things (IoT) into public safety infrastructure introduces critical preventative measures and rapid threat detection capabilities~\cite{simon2004}. Automated acoustic gunshot detection serves as a foundational layer of defense, autonomously identifying and locating firearm discharges in real-time. By continuously analyzing environmental audio at the edge, decentralized sensor networks generate immediate telemetry for emergency responders, drastically reducing dispatch latency. Operating strictly within the acoustic domain, these edge-sensing topologies preserve civilian privacy by circumventing pervasive video surveillance across urban grids, educational campuses, and wildlife reserves.

Existing methodologies for acoustic gunshot detection have historically relied upon digital signal processing and heuristic energy thresholding, techniques that remain notoriously susceptible to environmental noise. Foundational studies characterized gunshot acoustics as the convolution of a supersonic projectile shockwave and prolonged muzzle blast decay~\cite{maher2007}. While early systems employed conventional machine learning on Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) and commercial systems stream raw audio to centralized servers, recent baselines demonstrate the viability of localized inference on resource-constrained platforms, such as the Raspberry Pi~\cite{valero2020}. Concurrently, specialized acoustic datasets~\cite{dib2023, salamon2014} have enabled spectrogram-based convolutional networks in constrained environments~\cite{saha2025}.

Despite advances in audio classification, significant research gaps persist in deploying robust acoustic anomaly detectors onto low-cost edge hardware. Most models are trained on studio-quality datasets and fail when exposed to real-world microphone streams afflicted by transducer domain shifts, DC rail bias, thermal ADC hiss, and acoustic clipping. Furthermore, standard systems frequently trigger false alarms on percussive imposters (such as fireworks, door slams, and thunder) due to inadequate temporal analysis of ballistic reverberation decay. To resolve these challenges, this paper presents a hardware-aware edge sensing architecture featuring inline signal conditioning (sample-mean DC stripping and energy-gated scaling), an expanded 750\,ms operational window to encapsulate reverberant decay, and Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) to stabilize dynamic transducer fluctuations.

The primary contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \text{Decentralized Edge Architecture:} An autonomous edge pipeline on Raspberry Pi~4 executing localized inference with zero raw audio streaming, guaranteeing civilian privacy.
    \item \text{Hardware-Resilient Preprocessing:} Inline signal conditioning protocols (DC offset removal, energy-gated scaling, and saturation regularization) tailored for low-cost electret sensors.
    \item \text{TinyML Neural Benchmark:} Systematic profiling of 1D, 2D, and recurrent architectures under INT8 quantization~\cite{banbury2021}, highlighting an Enhanced 2D CNN with 0.09\,ms latency and a 124\,KB footprint.
    \item \text{Transducer Invariance via PCEN:} Validation that PCEN front-ends maintain stable detection recall (61\%) across severe acoustic distortion, frequency roll-off, and gain fluctuations.
    \item \text{Ensemble and Spatial Consensus:} A multi-model fusion strategy achieving 77\% unseen gunshot recall, coupled with a multi-node spatial consensus protocol to suppress localized false alarms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Acoustic gunshot detection has evolved from centralized military counter-sniper arrays to commercial urban acoustic sensor networks and localized edge IoT devices. Foundational works established the physics of ballistic acoustic impulses, modeling the shockwave conical Mach wave and spherical muzzle blast decay~\cite{maher2007}. Early wireless sensor network deployments, such as the countersniper architecture by Simon et al.~\cite{simon2004}, utilized multi-node time-of-arrival (TOA) telemetry to triangulate shooter positions without transmitting continuous audio waveforms. Concurrently, embedded acoustic sensors utilizing single-board computers have demonstrated the feasibility of on-device detection using convolutional neural networks~\cite{valero2020}.

The emergence of standardized open audio corpuses has catalyzed deep learning for environmental acoustic sensing. Calibrated firearm recordings, such as the multi-caliber dataset curated by Kabealo et al.~\cite{dib2023}, provide controlled multi-orientation ballistic samples. Urban sound corpuses, including UrbanSound8K~\cite{salamon2014}, ESC-50~\cite{piczak2015}, and FSD50K~\cite{fonseca2021}, alongside AudioSet~\cite{gemmeke2017}, have established standard benchmarks for environmental noise and percussive imposter events. For feature representation, deep convolutional models operating on log-mel spectrograms have demonstrated high efficacy across bioacoustic and environmental tasks~\cite{hershey2017, saha2025}, while 1D raw waveform convolutions have been explored for capturing sub-millisecond acoustic transients~\cite{dai2017}. To address non-stationary environmental acoustic noise, Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) was formulated by Lostanlen et al.~\cite{lostanlen2019, lostanlen2019taslp}, replacing static logarithmic scaling with adaptive temporal automatic gain control. On resource-constrained edge platforms, TinyML frameworks and integer quantization standards~\cite{banbury2021, david2021, cerutti2021} define the operational limits of microcontroller and single-board inference. However, existing literature primarily evaluates models under pristine laboratory conditions or relies on continuous cloud streaming, leaving open the challenge of autonomous, hardware-resilient detection on low-cost edge sensors.

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system_model}

The end-to-end system architecture operates entirely at the edge, converting raw acoustic waveforms into streamlined, actionable threat alerts. The block diagram of the proposed system model and edge processing pipeline is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig1_edge_pipeline.pdf}
\caption{End-to-end edge processing pipeline. The 750\,ms acoustic frame is conditioned and transformed into time-frequency representations directly on the embedded node. Quantized INT8 inference is executed locally, and only a streamlined detection telemetry packet is transmitted via the radio interface.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

The node's microphone continuously buffers ambient audio. When an impulsive transient exceeds the hardware energy threshold, a 750\,ms operational window is isolated. The raw waveform undergoes immediate signal conditioning to strip DC offsets and apply energy-gated peak scaling, intrinsically correcting for inexpensive hardware variances. The normalized signal is transformed into the time-frequency domain utilizing either log-mel spectrograms or PCEN. These extracted features are subsequently passed into the quantized INT8 neural network (e.g., Enhanced 2D CNN or CRNN-PCEN) executing on the Raspberry Pi's processor. Upon a positive detection, the system aggregates the confidence score and localized timestamp, transmitting only this highly streamlined telemetry to the central gateway, entirely bypassing the requirement to transmit raw audio payloads.

\section{Data Pipeline and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

The experimental audio corpus comprises approximately 80{,}000 samples, integrating the calibrated firearm dataset~\cite{dib2023}, 4{,}138 supplementary firearm recordings from diverse open-ground and indoor environments, and 6{,}230 ambient recordings (encompassing traffic, rain, construction, and speech) drawn from established environmental corpuses~\cite{salamon2014, piczak2015, fonseca2021}. Audio standardization and segmentation were performed with \texttt{librosa}, supported by manual auditory review on candidate subsets to ensure transient fidelity. Negative class files were filtered to discard clips whose crest factor and attack energy mimicked gunshot signatures. Crucially, while a synthetic 1:1 class balance artificially inflates baseline accuracy to 99--100\%, real-world streaming environments exhibit severe negative class skews (typically 1:10 to 1:50); hence, training and evaluation regimens incorporated heavily disproportionate negative distributions to replicate operational conditions.

Empirical evaluation of temporal window lengths demonstrated that while initial ballistic shockwaves occur over mere milliseconds, the ensuing reverberant decay carries the essential acoustic cues distinguishing genuine gunfire from sharp percussive knocks. Because 250\,ms windows prematurely truncate this decay, all models adopt a fixed \textbf{750\,ms} window (16{,}537 samples at 22{,}050\,Hz). For continuous streaming, a sliding buffer operates with a 187.5\,ms hop length (75\% overlap), guaranteeing that any transient impinging on a buffer boundary is captured within at least one complete analysis frame. Window alignment on training instances was anchored using spectral flux across STFT bins, peak absolute amplitude, or a localized energy envelope to prevent phase truncation.

\section{Hardware Conditioning for Edge Deployment}
\label{sec:conditioning}

The isolated 750\,ms frame requires stringent pre-conditioning prior to neural network ingestion (Stage~2 in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Transitioning from high-fidelity studio environments to real-world edge deployment introduces three primary signal distortions that must be mitigated algorithmically.

First, inexpensive electret microphones operating on standard 3.3\,V or 5\,V voltage rails exhibit inherent DC biasing, resting tens of millivolts off the true zero-crossing. This offset is systematically eradicated by subtracting the sample mean:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{clean}}[n] = y[n] - \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} y[i].
    \label{eq:dc_strip}
\end{equation}

Second, standard peak scaling ($y/\max(|y|)$) is mathematically destructive on quiescent windows. A peak value near $0.002$ will inadvertently stretch inherent ADC analog hiss across the entire digital dynamic range. To circumvent this, scaling is applied exclusively via an energy-gated function when the window possesses sufficient root-mean-square (RMS) volume:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{cond}} =
    \begin{cases}
        y / \max(|y|) & \text{if RMS} \ge 0.005, \\
        y & \text{if RMS} < 0.005.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:energy_gate}
\end{equation}

Third, proximate firearm discharges routinely exceed 120\,dB SPL, physically saturating the microphone capsule and resulting in hard digital clipping. To ensure robustness, synthetic hard clipping was aggressively integrated into the training augmentation pipeline, forcing the convolutional filters to recognize the truncated rise-time characteristic of a saturated waveform.

\section{Model Architectures}
\label{sec:models}

Initial baseline evaluations utilizing logistic regression, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Random Forests operating on MFCC summaries achieved 99--100\% accuracy on balanced datasets. However, these models exhibited catastrophic failure during continuous live microphone streaming, as temporal averaging obliterates microsecond ballistic shockwave signatures. The four deployable neural network architectures detailed below share the hardware conditioning pipeline outlined in Section~\ref{sec:conditioning}. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the overarching architectural topologies.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig_architectures.pdf}
\caption{Modular topologies of the four deployed edge neural network architectures.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

The baseline 1D CNN processes the raw 16,537-sample waveform directly. Its initial convolutional kernel is 80 samples wide (approximately $3.6$\,ms at 22,050\,Hz)~\cite{dai2017}, enabling the primary layer to encapsulate rapid acoustic attacks. However, this architecture exhibits severe overfitting to training microphones: while hold-out recall achieves 99.7\%, real-world recall on external recordings collapses to 30\%.

The baseline 2D CNN analyzes a 64-band log-mel spectrogram (130 frames, FFT size 512, hop length 128)~\cite{hershey2017}. To avoid ReLU gradient death across negative decibel domains (which stalled initial unscaled prototypes at 0\% recall), spectrogram inputs are normalized into $[0,1]$ via:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{norm}} = (S_{\mathrm{dB}} + 80) / 80.
    \label{eq:norm_db}
\end{equation}
Max-pooling operations are omitted from final convolutional blocks to retain high-frequency attack transients.

The Enhanced 2D CNN adopts a similar methodology with reduced filter widths, augmented by MixUp~\cite{zhang2018}, SpecAugment~\cite{park2019}, speed perturbation~\cite{ko2015}, and an auxiliary classification head trained to output an anomaly score. (A waveform-based dual-head 1D prototype was deprecated after INT8 quantization caused catastrophic weight saturation, falsely flagging all windows as positive; bounded $[0,1]$ 2D spectrograms quantize deterministically without saturation).

The CRNN architecture supplants the static log-mel spectrogram with PCEN~\cite{lostanlen2019,lostanlen2019taslp}, defined as:
\begin{equation}
    P[f,t] = \left( \frac{S[f,t]}{(\epsilon + M[f,t])^{\alpha}} + \delta \right)^{r} - \delta^{r},
    \label{eq:pcen}
\end{equation}
where $M[f,t]$ represents a single-pole IIR temporal smoothing of $S[f,t]$. By configuring $\alpha$ near $0.98$, stationary frequency bands asymptotically settle near unity, rendering the output resilient to physical microphone gain swings ($g^{0.04}$). A bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)~\cite{cho2014} with 64 units per direction subsequently analyzes the temporal frame sequence to reject sustained non-ballistic tones.

Preliminary feasibility checks on Cortex-M sound event configurations~\cite{cerutti2021} demonstrated that while 1D MFCC networks yielded catastrophic false positive rates during streaming (33 of 47 silence windows falsely flagged), 2D spectrogram models reliably rejected environmental anomalies, confirming the necessity of time-frequency representations.

\section{Experimental Setup and Metrics}
\label{sec:setup}

All empirical neural metrics reported herein utilize strictly exported INT8 quantized models~\cite{david2021}. Inference latency was established over 100 timed iterations executing on the Raspberry Pi~4, with CPU utilization strictly governed at 45--50\% utilizing 2\,GB of allocated RAM~\cite{banbury2021}. The TensorFlow Lite runtime leveraged the exact integer arithmetic path designated for embedded target boards.

The experimental evaluation encompasses three distinct datasets to rigorously assess model generalization. The balanced hold-out dataset consists of 1,442 clips of 750\,ms duration (721 gunshots, 721 ambient/imposter) derived from the primary cleaned corpus. The external dataset contains 75 recordings procured independently of the primary corpus: 20 firearms, 20 imposters, and 35 ambient tracks. The primary validation set features 300 entirely unseen recordings: 100 genuine gunshots, 100 ambient files, and 100 highly challenging percussive imposters (e.g., fireworks, door slams, thunder, drum beats).

Evaluation metrics are defined as follows: \textit{Gunshot recall} is the fraction of genuine firearm files exceeding the detection threshold. \textit{Ambient specificity} represents the fraction of rain, traffic, and speech files correctly rejected below the threshold. \textit{Imposter rejection} indicates the successful suppression of percussive non-firearm events. \textit{Latency} denotes the computational time required to score a single 750\,ms operational window. Given the system's operational hop length of 187.5\,ms, any model exceeding this latency threshold fundamentally violates real-time processing constraints. Unless explicitly stated, the default classification threshold ($\tau$) is defined as $0.50$.

Furthermore, \textit{peak-centered scoring} evaluates a singular 750\,ms acoustic slice perfectly centered upon the maximum waveform amplitude, whereas \textit{sliding scoring} continuously transverses the window across the entire audio file. Naturally, the sliding approach identifies more obscured shots but concomitantly increases the baseline false positive rate.

\subsection{Ensemble Architecture}
The Enhanced 2D CNN, the CRNN-PCEN, and the Baseline 1D CNN were fused into a multi-model ensemble. The logic assumes a positive detection if any constituent model exceeds its respective threshold, subsequently gated by a rapid spectral heuristic check (analyzing energy below 200\,Hz, energy above 4\,kHz, and spectral centroid shifts). Table~\ref{tab:ensemble} and Figure~\ref{fig:cm} report the performance on the 300-clip dataset. This ensemble approach successfully elevates recall from 67\% to 77\% (capturing 77 out of 100 true gunshots). False positives remain a challenge: 44 out of 100 hard imposters and 20 out of 100 ambient files triggered false alerts. The total ensemble memory footprint is approximately 715\,KB, requiring a combined 17\,ms execution time, which remains well within the strict operational hop boundary.

The performance augmentation is driven by complementary failure modes rather than redundant feature extraction. For instance, on the file \texttt{127724.wav}, the Enhanced 2D CNN generates a low 21.1\% confidence, whereas the CRNN correctly identifies the event with 99.3\% confidence. Conversely, on \texttt{170167.wav}, the CRNN fails entirely (0\% confidence) while the Enhanced 2D CNN triggers accurately at 99.6\%. For an initial, resource-constrained sensor node, the standalone Enhanced 2D model offers the optimal balance of speed and precision. \section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study conclusively demonstrates that while classical machine learning classifiers achieve near-perfect accuracy on balanced hold-out datasets, they fail catastrophically during live deployment because temporal averaging irreversibly destroys the microsecond shockwave signatures intrinsic to firearms. The architectural transition from 1D MFCC classifiers to 2D spectrogram-based CNNs yielded substantial improvements in the rejection of ambient noise and percussive imposters. Evaluated against 300 highly complex unseen recordings, the proposed Enhanced 2D CNN achieved 67\% sliding gunshot recall and 99\% peak ambient specificity, executing in a remarkable 0.09\,ms while occupying only 124\,KB of flash memory. Furthermore, the implementation of a PCEN-CRNN architecture demonstrated unparalleled resilience, maintaining stable recall under severe simulated microphone distortion with zero algorithmic degradation, thereby validating PCEN as a superior domain-shift normalizer for acoustic edge applications.

A critical physical limitation was also identified during empirical testing: a solitary microphone is inherently incapable of distinguishing between aerial mortar fireworks and genuine gunfire, as both events generate virtually identical shockwave rise-times and reverberation profiles in the time domain. Resolving this acoustic ambiguity necessitates the future integration of spatial multi-microphone arrays.

The subsequent phases of this research will prioritize the following hardware integration tasks:

\textbf{1) Physical Edge Deployment.} Systematically deploy the Enhanced 2D CNN INT8 quantized model onto a Raspberry Pi~4 architecture to validate deterministic edge timing, followed by a direct port of the inference engine to the highly constrained Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense (utilizing the on-board ST MP34DT05 PDM microphone).

\textbf{2) Duty-Cycled Deep Sleep and Minimalist Telemetry.} The edge node will maintain a deep sleep state (drawing approximately 5\,$\mu$A) until a coarse analog energy threshold triggers a hardware interrupt~\cite{cerutti2021}. A single inference cycle is subsequently executed before returning to sleep. Upon a positive threshold breach, the node will transmit a minimalist packet structure comprising: node ID (1~byte), timestamp (4~bytes), detection flag (1~bit), and confidence score (1~byte). This protocol restricts radio transmission time to under 1.5\,ms per trigger event. Negative inferences trigger no transmission, ensuring that raw audio is strictly confined to the local node to guarantee absolute privacy.

\textbf{3) Multi-Node Spatial Consensus.} To mitigate false positives generated by localized percussive anomalies (e.g., a proximal hand clap), the central gateway will elevate a system-wide alert exclusively when a spatial consensus of $K \ge 3$ geographically distributed nodes transmit concurrent detection flags within a highly constrained acoustic propagation window (approximately 100\,ms for a 34\,m sensor spacing). This topological approach effectively filters localized imposters without compromising network bandwidth or streaming raw audio.

\textbf{4) Self-Healing Network Diagnostics and Dashboard Integration.} Embedded nodes will autonomously transmit periodic diagnostic telemetry encompassing battery degradation, sensor baseline drift, and radio link integrity. A centralized dashboard deployed at the gateway will continuously aggregate this telemetry and log every positive trigger, establishing a comprehensive, immutable audit trail for post-incident forensic analysis.
